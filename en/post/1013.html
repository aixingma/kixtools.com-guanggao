How We Ran the Test

We pushed the same roughly thirty-minute on-demand playlist through four different pipelines and judged each on setup effort, wall-clock speed, output fidelity, and how much it respects your privacy. No single route won outright — each has a territory where it is clearly the right tool, and the survey below shows where the borders fall.

Route 1: In-Browser Converter

Our online M3U8 to MP4 converter runs an FFmpeg build compiled to WebAssembly, entirely inside the page. Drop in the stream address, hit start, and the finished file lands in your downloads folder when the progress bar fills. Nothing ever leaves your machine. The constraint is the browser's security model: sources that withhold CORS headers simply will not load, and very long files test the tab's memory limits. For sub-half-hour clips from cooperative servers, though, it is hard to beat for convenience.

Route 2: FFmpeg in the Terminal

The command-line build does everything the wasm version does, with no CORS ceiling and no memory ceiling. Two recipes cover most situations:

# Lossless remux — copies the tracks untouched, done in seconds
ffmpeg -i "https://example.com/index.m3u8" -c copy -movflags +faststart output.mp4

# Full re-encode when maximum device compatibility matters
ffmpeg -i "https://example.com/index.m3u8" -c:v libx264 -preset medium -crf 20 -c:a aac output.mp4

The remux is the right default: it never touches the encoded streams, so quality is bit-identical and speed limited only by your bandwidth. The +faststart flag moves the MP4's index to the file's head, so playback starts before the download finishes. Full re-encoding costs dramatically more time and should be reserved for players that choke on the original codec layout. AES-128 encrypted playlists decrypt automatically whenever the key file is publicly reachable.

Route 3: VLC and Desktop Apps

VLC hides a converter behind Media → Open Network Stream: paste the URL, tick Convert, choose an MP4 profile, and pick a destination. Various GUI converter apps wrap the same libraries with friendlier screens. The upside is a point-and-click experience; the downside is thinner control — profiles expose a fraction of FFmpeg's knobs, and debugging a failed conversion usually means opening a terminal anyway. Fine for the occasional file.

Route 4: Real-Time Capture

Where none of the above applies — protected platforms, streams that hide their playlists behind obfuscated JavaScript — recording the screen while the video plays is the catch-all. OBS Studio or your OS's built-in recorder will faithfully preserve whatever the display shows. It is the slowest route (one hour of footage costs one hour), produces the largest files, and caps quality at your monitor's output, but it succeeds on sources that defeat every direct method.

Whichever route you choose, keep one habit: convert once, store the MP4, and treat the playlist file itself as disposable. More background on finding and saving streams is in our guide to saving m3u8 streams locally.
